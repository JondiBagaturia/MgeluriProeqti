SPHEREx Blink

Blink NASA SPHEREx epochs at any sky position to spot moving or variable objects — inspired by WiseView.

Search

Large FOV — this will be slower.

Detectors

Caps exposures combined per epoch (fewer = faster to fetch, slightly noisier). Low=~5, Mid=~15, High=all available — spread evenly across the epoch's time span either way.

Discovery

Differences are not PSF-matched and use nearest-pixel alignment: bright stationary stars also leave residuals. Compare a candidate with its neighbours before reading anything into it.

Display

Asinh uses fixed levels (median / 99.8th percentile, computed across the whole loaded sequence) — the black/white sliders are inactive in this mode.

Spectrum (bonus)

Click in the viewer for aperture flux (mJy, ±1σ, r = 9″, no aperture correction) vs wavelength, across all 6 detectors. Clicking on the crosshair of a proper-motion target follows its motion per exposure. Points are coloured by date. SPHEREx does not see all wavelengths at once, so a variable star's points from different dates are not one spectrum. Slow: it re-queries IRSA.

Enter a position above (try "Barnard's star", "WISE J085510.83-071442.5", or "269.452 4.693") and press Go. For typed coordinates the app asks CatWISE2020 whether a known fast mover passed through them, and otherwise treats them as fixed. Entering a fast-moving object's name is more reliable (WISE 0855 is now ~2′ from its 2010 coordinates).

Light curve (aperture flux at the target's predicted position per frame, mJy ±1σ, this detector only. Hollow markers = measurement refused (masked/saturated/off-image). Error bars are statistical only: frames of a constant star differ by ~3–5% (measured on field stars). Each frame also samples a different wavelength within the detector (see λ in the frame label), so differences can be spectral rather than variability.)
